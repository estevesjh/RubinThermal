\section{Introduction}

The M1M3 thermal control system uses a setpoint rate limit of
0.7\,\textdegree C/h to prevent excessive thermal stress on the mirror.
Previous analysis showed that this hard constraint is the dominant bottleneck
for control performance: increasing the rate limit from 0.7 to
1.0\,\textdegree C/h yields an 18\% improvement in overnight RMS---larger than
any forecast or controller upgrade at fixed rate limit.

Figure~\ref{fig:cooling_rate} shows that 49\% of observing nights have at
least one 1-hour period where the inside temperature changes faster than
0.7\,\textdegree C/h---the hard limit prevents the mirror from tracking these
events.  This report investigates replacing the hard rate clip with soft
penalties that allow occasional rate violations when the tracking benefit
outweighs the rate cost.  We compare two soft control strategies---PID with
cubic rate damping and MPC with an asymmetric quartic rate penalty---against
the hard-clipped baselines (Three-Phase, Hard MPC, Hard PID).

\section{Method}

\subsection{Hard Baseline Controllers}

The hard rate limit clips the setpoint change to
$|\Delta u| \leq \Delta u_\mathrm{max} = 0.7 \cdot \Delta t$ at each timestep,
regardless of the controller's desired output.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Three-Phase}: heuristic weighted lookahead (3\,h kernel) with
    rate compensation.
  \item \textbf{Hard MPC}: model predictive control with box constraints
    enforcing the rate limit inside the optimizer.
  \item \textbf{Hard PID}: feedforward + PID ($K_p=3$, $K_i=0.05$, $K_d=0.3$)
    with external hard clip.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Soft PID Controller}

The Soft PID computes the setpoint as:
\begin{equation}
  T_\mathrm{sp} = T_\mathrm{ff} + K_p e + K_i \int e\,dt
    - K_d \dot{T}_\mathrm{mirror} - K_\mathrm{soft}\, v^3 \Delta u_\mathrm{max}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $T_\mathrm{ff} = T_\mathrm{amb} - 0.3$\,\textdegree C is the
    feedforward tracking term (direct, no rate limiting);
  \item $e = T_\mathrm{ff} - T_\mathrm{mirror}$ is the tracking error;
  \item $\dot{T}_\mathrm{mirror}$ is the derivative-on-measurement term
    (prevents setpoint kick);
  \item $v = \Delta u / \Delta u_\mathrm{max}$ is the normalized setpoint rate;
  \item $K_\mathrm{soft}\, v^3$ is the cubic rate damping: gentle at low rates,
    harsh above the limit.
\end{itemize}

No external rate clip is applied.  The cubic term creates a soft wall at
$v = 1$ (the 0.7\,\textdegree C/h boundary).  Three scenarios were tuned via
grid search over $(K_p, K_i, K_d, K_\mathrm{soft})$ on 166 nights:

\subsection{Bias-Corrected Asymmetric Quartic MPC}

The MPC optimizes $N = 8$ future setpoints (2\,h horizon) with two terms:
\begin{equation}
  J = \underbrace{\sum_{k} (w_k \cdot \beta^k) \, e_k^2}_{\text{time-discounted tracking}}
    + \underbrace{\left( \alpha_\mathrm{heat} \sum_{k} [\max(v_k, 0)]^4
           + \alpha_\mathrm{cool} \sum_{k} [\max(-v_k, 0)]^4 \right) \Delta u_\mathrm{max}}_{\text{asymmetric rate penalty}}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $w_k = \alpha_\mathrm{warm}$ if $e_k > 0$, else $\alpha_\mathrm{cold}$
    (asymmetric tracking cost, $\alpha_\mathrm{warm}/\alpha_\mathrm{cold} = 4$);
  \item $\beta^k$: time-decay discount ($\beta = 0.9$), weighting near-term
    tracking more than distant predictions where the forecast degrades;
  \item $v_k = \Delta u_k / \Delta u_\mathrm{max}$ is the normalized setpoint rate;
  \item $v_k^4$: quartic wall---flat near zero, brick wall above $|v| = 1$;
  \item $\alpha_\mathrm{heat} > \alpha_\mathrm{cool}$: heating is penalized more
    than cooling (cold mirror is acceptable).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Forecast bias correction.}
The Ridge forecast has night-specific systematic bias (up to
$\pm$0.5\,\textdegree C on difficult nights).  A \texttt{BiasCorrectedProvider}
wraps the forecast provider and corrects this online:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Logs forecast predictions at a fixed lead time (1\,h ahead) as they
    are issued.
  \item When actuals become available, computes the forecast error.
  \item Tracks the error with a median filter + EMA ($k_f = 0.7$).
  \item Applies the correction only when the bias is \emph{stable}
    (spread $< 0.25$\,\textdegree C in recent errors) and \emph{significant}
    ($|$bias$| > 0.15$\,\textdegree C dead zone).
\end{enumerate}
This corrects persistent forecast bias without affecting nights where the
forecast is already accurate.

\subsection{Metrics}

All metrics are computed for $t \geq 2$\,h after sunset on 166 nights with
Ridge forecast:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RMS}: root mean square of
    $T_\mathrm{mirror} - (T_\mathrm{inside} - 0.3)$;
  \item \textbf{$\pm$0.3\,\textdegree C}: fraction of time within target band;
  \item \textbf{Violation rate}: fraction of timesteps where the actual setpoint
    rate exceeds 0.75\,\textdegree C/h (0.7 + 0.05 margin);
  \item \textbf{p95 rate}: 95th percentile of $|\Delta u / \Delta t|$;
  \item \textbf{Max rate}: worst-case observed rate.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Soft PID: dramatic improvement, high rate cost}

The Soft PID with a hardware rate ceiling of 2.1\,\textdegree C/h
(3$\times$ the nominal limit) achieves excellent tracking while bounding
peak rates to a physically realistic level.

The Aggressive scenario ($K_p = 2$, $K_i = 0.1$, $K_d = 0.3$) achieves
RMS = 0.40\,\textdegree C, 91\% within $\pm$0.3\,\textdegree C, and 97\%
within $\pm$0.5\,\textdegree C---a dramatic improvement over all hard-clipped
controllers---with 34\% of timesteps exceeding 0.75\,\textdegree C/h.

The Balanced scenario ($K_\mathrm{soft} = 0.15$) uses cubic damping to
keep violations under 21\%, achieving 76\% within $\pm$0.3\,\textdegree C---still
better than Three-Phase (72\%).

With the 2.1\,\textdegree C/h ceiling, all PID scenarios have bounded peak
rates, addressing the previous concern about extreme rate excursions on
difficult nights.

\subsection{Bias-Corrected MPC: bounded rates, adaptive correction}

The bias-corrected asymmetric quartic MPC combines three innovations:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Asymmetric quartic rate penalty}: separate costs for heating
    ($\alpha_\mathrm{heat}$) and cooling ($\alpha_\mathrm{cool}$), reflecting
    the physical preference for cold.  The quartic wall naturally limits
    maximum rate to $\sim$2.1\,\textdegree C/h.
  \item \textbf{Time-decaying tracking} ($\beta = 0.9$): near-term forecasts
    are weighted more heavily, reducing the influence of degraded long-horizon
    predictions.
  \item \textbf{Online bias correction}: a median+EMA filter tracks the
    forecast error at 1\,h lead time and subtracts the persistent component
    from all future predictions.  The correction activates only when the
    bias is stable and exceeds 0.15\,\textdegree C (dead zone), leaving
    accurate forecasts unchanged.
\end{enumerate}

The MPC Aggressive scenario achieves RMS = 0.37\,\textdegree C,
83\% within $\pm$0.3\,\textdegree C---beating Three-Phase (72\%) by 10 points
while keeping peak rates bounded at 2.1\,\textdegree C/h.  The Conservative
scenario ($\alpha_\mathrm{cool} = 2.0$) restricts cooling rate more than
heating, achieving only 10\% violation with p95 = 0.84\,\textdegree C/h and
max rate below 2.0\,\textdegree C/h.

\subsection{PID vs MPC: different failure modes}

The two soft controllers have complementary strengths:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Soft PID}: best tracking accuracy, purely reactive, but
    unbounded peak rates and no forecast awareness.  The PID must be paired
    with a hardware rate ceiling ($\sim$2.1\,\textdegree C/h) to prevent
    extreme excursions.
  \item \textbf{Asymmetric Quartic MPC}: naturally bounded peak rates
    ($\leq$2.1\,\textdegree C/h), forecast-aware planning, and
    physics-informed asymmetry between heating and cooling.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The rate limit is still the bottleneck}

The central finding is confirmed: allowing even modest rate violations
(10--20\% of timesteps exceeding 0.75\,\textdegree C/h) yields
significant improvement in tracking accuracy compared to hard-clipped
controllers.  The 95th percentile rate at $\sim$10\% violation is
$\sim$1.0\,\textdegree C/h---only 1.4$\times$ the nominal limit.

\subsection{Alternative: logarithmic barrier for hard boundaries}

If the hardware imposes a strict maximum rate that must never be exceeded
(e.g., due to thermal stress limits on the glycol system), the quartic
penalty can be replaced with a logarithmic barrier:
\begin{equation}
  J_\mathrm{barrier} = -\mu_\mathrm{heat} \sum_k \ln(1 - v_k)
                       -\mu_\mathrm{cool} \sum_k \ln(1 + v_k)
\end{equation}
where $v_k = \Delta u_k / (\Delta u_\mathrm{max} \cdot v_\mathrm{max})$
is normalized to $(-1, 1)$ and $v_\mathrm{max}$ sets the absolute ceiling
(e.g., $v_\mathrm{max} = 1.5$ limits rate to 1.05\,\textdegree C/h).
The barrier goes to infinity as $|v| \to 1$, creating an impassable wall
that the optimizer cannot cross.  Preliminary tests show that the asymmetric
log barrier with $\mu_\mathrm{heat} = 0.2$, $\mu_\mathrm{cool} = 0.1$
achieves 87\% within $\pm$0.3\,\textdegree C with $<$10\% violation and a
guaranteed maximum rate of 0.99\,\textdegree C/h---comparable to the quartic
but with mathematically guaranteed rate bounds.

\section{Conclusions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Replacing the hard 0.7\,\textdegree C/h rate clip with soft penalties
    yields significant improvement in tracking accuracy at the cost of
    modest rate violations.

  \item The \textbf{Soft PID Aggressive} ($K_p = 2$, $K_i = 0.1$, $K_d = 0.3$)
    with a 2.1\,\textdegree C/h ceiling achieves the best $\pm$0.3\,\textdegree C
    (91\%) with 34\% violation.

  \item The \textbf{Bias-Corrected Asymmetric Quartic MPC}
    ($\alpha_\mathrm{warm}/\alpha_\mathrm{cold}=4$, $\beta=0.9$) achieves
    the best RMS (0.37\,\textdegree C) with naturally bounded rates
    ($\leq$2.1\,\textdegree C/h).  The online bias correction based on
    median+EMA filtering of 1\,h-ahead forecast errors adaptively removes
    persistent forecast bias without affecting accurate nights.

  \item The practical recommendation depends on the violation budget:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item $\leq$10\% violation: \textbf{MPC Conservative}
        ($\alpha_\mathrm{cool}=2.0$; 76\% $\pm$0.3\,\textdegree C,
        p95 = 0.84\,\textdegree C/h, max $< 2.0$).
      \item $\leq$33\% violation: \textbf{MPC Aggressive}
        (83\% $\pm$0.3\,\textdegree C, p95 = 1.52\,\textdegree C/h).
      \item $\leq$34\% violation: \textbf{Soft PID Aggressive}
        (91\% $\pm$0.3\,\textdegree C).
      \item Zero violation: \textbf{Three-Phase}
        (72\% $\pm$0.3\,\textdegree C).
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
